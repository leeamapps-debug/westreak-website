Delete your WeStreak account

This page explains how to delete your account in WeStreak: Habit Tracker for 2, made by Leeam Applications. You can do it in the app in under a minute, or by email if you no longer have the app.

Before you start: cancel your subscription

Deleting your WeStreak account does not cancel an App Store or Google Play subscription. If you pay for Premium, cancel it first, or Apple or Google will keep charging you.

On iPhone: open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions, then WeStreak, then Cancel Subscription. Or go to apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions.

On Android: open the Google Play app, tap your profile picture, then Payments and subscriptions, then Subscriptions, then WeStreak, then Cancel subscription. Or go to play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions.

To ask for a refund, use Apple's Report a Problem page at reportaproblem.apple.com, or Google Play's refund request page.

Delete your account in the app

  1. Open WeStreak and sign in.
  2. Go to Settings, then tap Delete account.
  3. Read what will be deleted, then tap I understand, continue.
  4. Tap Delete my account.

Your account is deleted straight away.

Delete your account without the app

You do not need to reinstall WeStreak.

  1. Email info@leeamapplications.com with the subject Delete my WeStreak account.
  2. Tell us the email address you use for WeStreak. If you used Sign in with Apple with Hide My Email and do not know that address, tell us your first name and roughly when you joined, and we will help.
  3. To make sure the request really comes from you, we will email a code to the address on your account. Reply with the code.
  4. We will delete your account within 7 days of receiving the code, and email you to confirm when it is done.

What we delete

When your account is deleted, we remove from our live systems your account details, meaning your name, email address, password hash, and your Apple or Google sign-in link; your habits, icons, reminder times, daily ticks, breaks and grid colour; the nudges and cheers you sent and received; your invite codes and links, and any partner name you typed during setup; your notification settings, time zone and push token; your subscription status in WeStreak; and our security records about your account. If you are not paired, your pair information, such as your streak, pair days and recaps, goes too. If you used Sign in with Apple, we also tell Apple to disconnect WeStreak from your Apple ID.

What we keep, and for how long

  1. Shared history with a partner. If you are paired, the habits you shared are archived so your partner keeps their own history. Your ticks are deleted. What is left is deleted once your partner no longer has an account either.
  2. Backups. Copies in our encrypted backups are overwritten within to be confirmed before WeStreak launches. We never restore deleted accounts from them.
  3. Anonymous usage statistics and crash reports. These are not linked to your account, name or email, so we cannot single out yours. They are deleted automatically after to be confirmed before WeStreak launches and to be confirmed before WeStreak launches.
  4. Subscription records. We keep none. Apple or Google keep the records of your payments under their own policies.
  5. Emails with us. If you emailed us, we keep that conversation for up to 2 years, unless we need it longer for a legal claim.

What happens to your partner

If you are paired, the pair ends. Your partner keeps their own account and their own habits, but your name, habits, ticks and messages disappear from their app. If you were paying for Premium, your partner goes back to the free plan. If your partner was paying, nothing changes for their subscription.

Want to delete only some data?

You can do some things without deleting your account. Archive a habit in the app, which takes it off your grid. Unpair from your partner in Settings. Switch off anonymous usage statistics in Settings. Turn off notifications in your phone's settings. Or email us to ask us to delete or correct particular information, such as an archived habit's history.
